Act 1 - The Challenge

HOP Electric needed a digital-first strategy to match their premium brand

HOP Electric was launching their flagship electric bike model but had no online sales infrastructure. The brand faced three critical problems:

Challenge 01
No online buying path
All sales were dealer-only. Customers couldn't buy online, leaving revenue on the table.
Challenge 02
Product specs unclear
No clear way to communicate bike variants, specs, and configurations to buyers.
Challenge 03
Dealer network invisible
Customers couldn't find nearby dealers. No booking bridge between online and physical experience.
The brand was premium but the buying experience was fragmented. We needed to create a seamless digital-first platform that celebrated the product and made buying effortless.

Act 2 - Strategy & Discovery

I embedded with engineering and leadership to understand the product inside out

I spent the first 2 weeks working directly with HOP's bike engineering team, CEO, and CTO to understand three things: the product itself, the target customer, and the business vision.

Understanding the Product

Working with the engineering team, I learned every detail of the HOP bike: frame materials, motor specifications, battery performance, weight distribution, and the engineering philosophy behind each variant. This depth gave me the credibility to communicate specs in a way engineers could respect and buyers could understand.

I identified three core variants based on rider use-case: Urban (daily commute), Performance (weekend adventures), and Cargo (business/lifestyle). Each needed its own story.

Connecting with Leadership

Conversations with the CEO and CTO revealed the business strategy: premium positioning, rapid market penetration, and using digital to reduce dealer pressure. They wanted online booking to drive trial without cannibalizing dealer relationships — a delicate balance.

This shaped every design decision. The platform had to make buying online feel exclusive and premium, not transactional.

Key Insight 01
Buyers are research-heavy
E-bike buyers spend 8+ weeks researching before purchase. Specs, reviews, and videos matter more than hype.
Key Insight 02
Trust = proximity
People want to see and feel the bike before committing. The dealer locator wasn't a feature—it was the primary call-to-action.

Act 3 - Design Approach

Built a platform that showcases the product, locates dealers, and enables booking

The design centered on four pillars: educate, locate, book, and delight.

Pillar 01
Product Education
Detailed spec pages for each variant with interactive comparisons, video walkthroughs, and technical deep-dives.
Pillar 02
Dealer Discovery
Location-based dealer finder with inventory, availability, and direct booking from dealer profiles.
Pillar 03
Online Booking
Two pathways: direct online purchase or book a test ride at a nearby dealer.
Pillar 04
Dealer Portal
Backend management system for dealers to track inventory, bookings, and customer inquiries in real-time.

Act 4 - The Solution

A seamless platform that turned online browsers into buyers

The final platform delivered five core experiences:

1. Interactive Product Configurator

Buyers could visualize their perfect HOP bike: choose color, components, and accessories. Real-time pricing updates showed the financial impact of every choice. Build-your-own created emotional investment before purchase.

2. Dealer Locator with Smart Filtering

GPS-powered dealer finder showing inventory, availability, and customer ratings. Users could filter by variant, location radius, and dealer type (showroom, service center, pop-up). Reduced friction from "find dealer" to "book test ride" to seconds.

3. Dual Booking Pathways

Digital buyers could complete purchase online with 2-3-day delivery coordination. Risk-averse buyers could book a test ride at their local dealer, turning the website into a dealer-generator without cannibalizing sales.

4. Dealer Dashboard

Real-time view of online inquiries, test ride bookings, and inventory across the HOP network. Dealers could respond to customers within hours, staying competitive with direct online sales.

5. Premium Brand Experience

Every pixel reflected HOP's premium positioning: high-res product photography, technical deep-dives, customer testimonials, and a trust-first checkout process. Buying online felt like a privilege, not a compromise.

Act 6 - Reflection

What made this project succeed

Deep product knowledge mattered. Spending time with the engineering team wasn't a nice-to-have—it was essential. I could speak the language of motors, battery chemistry, and frame geometry, which built credibility with the team and showed in the quality of specs on the website.

Alignment with leadership was everything. By understanding the CEO's vision and CTO's technical constraints early, I designed a solution that served everyone: customers got an easy buying experience, dealers got a competitive advantage, and HOP got sustainable revenue growth without channel conflict.

The dealer network wasn't the problem—it was the solution. Instead of treating dealers as obstacles to online sales, the platform made them partners. That mindset shift changed everything about how the website was designed and received.

If I did it again

I would invest even more in post-purchase experience. The website got people to "book" but the onboarding, delivery coordination, and early ownership experience is where lifetime value is won. An integration with HOP's CRM to track customer journey post-booking would have been valuable.
