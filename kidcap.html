Kidcap

Designing a Savings Experience for Families

+18% activation 4.8 NPS on onboarding 27% drop-off reduction

Kidcap is a fintech product designed to help families start saving together—while teaching kids the basics of money along the way. The challenge wasn't just building an onboarding flow. It was designing something that parents could trust with their money, kids could understand and stay engaged, and still meet strict financial and legal compliance requirements.

The Real Problem

At first glance, this looked like a simple onboarding redesign.

It wasn't.

The deeper I went, the more I realized this problem sat at the intersection of:

  • Finance (high trust, high risk)
  • Education (low attention, high complexity)
  • Family dynamics (multiple decision-makers)

The real problems were:

1. Trust Gap in Family Finance

Parents weren't just evaluating the product—they were asking:

"Can I trust this with my child's financial future?"

Unlike typical fintech apps:

  • Mistakes here feel irreversible
  • Users are more risk-averse
  • Any confusion = drop-off

This made clarity and transparency non-negotiable.

2. Mental Model Mismatch

Parents think in:

  • Returns
  • Risk
  • Long-term planning

Kids think in:

  • Rewards
  • Visual progress
  • Immediate outcomes

The product had to bridge two completely different mental models in one flow.

3. Compliance vs Experience Conflict

KYC, consent, and documentation flows created a major tension:

  • Compliance needs more information
  • Good UX needs less friction

This created a core design challenge: How do you reduce friction in a flow that legally cannot be simplified?

4. Drop-Off Wasn't About UI — It Was About Anxiety

Users weren't dropping off because of bad design.

They were dropping off because:

  • They didn't understand financial terms
  • They feared making the wrong decision
  • They felt overwhelmed midway

This turned onboarding into a confidence problem, not just a usability problem.

5. "Saving" Felt Abstract and Distant

For kids especially:

  • Saving money felt invisible
  • There was no immediate feedback
  • No emotional connection

Without motivation, the experience failed before it even started.

6. Parents Felt Alone in the Process

Most fintech apps assume: "User already knows what to do"

But here:

  • Parents needed guidance
  • They didn't want to "learn finance" first
  • They wanted help while doing

Users & Insight Shift

After research, one key shift changed everything:

This is not a fintech product. This is a guided family behavior product.

Approach

Instead of optimizing onboarding screens, I focused on:

  • Reducing fear
  • Increasing clarity
  • Creating shared motivation

Designing the Experience

Dual-Path Journey

I designed two synchronized flows:

  • Parent → decision-making, setup, compliance
  • Child → engagement, learning, motivation

Full journey map showing parent vs child flow + emotional states

Key Design Decisions

1. Reducing Anxiety Through Guidance

Instead of leaving users alone in complex steps:

  • Added contextual explanations
  • Introduced "mentor-like" guidance
  • Broke decisions into smaller chunks

Step with helper text explaining SIP in simple terms

2. Translating Finance Into Everyday Language

Financial jargon was a major blocker.

So instead of: "Start SIP"

We used: "Set up monthly savings for your child's goal"

Comparison of complex vs simplified language

3. Bridging Parent–Child Mental Models

To align both users:

  • Parents see structured financial inputs
  • Kids see visual goals and progress

Same system—different representation.

Parent dashboard vs child view

4. Making Progress Tangible

To fix the "abstract savings" problem:

  • Visual milestones
  • Goal tracking
  • Reinforcement feedback

Goal completion tracker with milestones

5. Designing Compliance as Part of the Journey

Instead of isolating KYC:

  • Integrated it into natural steps
  • Explained why each input is needed
  • Reduced perceived effort

Clean, step-by-step KYC UI

Testing & Iteration

Testing revealed something important:

Confusion wasn't about UI—it was about understanding.

Changes made:

  • Simplified language further
  • Reduced cognitive load in forms
  • Improved visual cues for kids

Impact

  • +18% activation
  • 4.8 onboarding NPS
  • 27% drop-off reduction

But more importantly:

Users moved from hesitation → confidence → action

What I Learned

  • UX in fintech is less about screens, more about trust systems
  • Solving for multiple users means solving for multiple mindsets
  • The biggest friction isn't interaction—it's uncertainty

Final Thought

Kidcap wasn't just about improving onboarding.

It was about solving a much harder problem:

Helping families feel safe, confident, and motivated to start saving together.
